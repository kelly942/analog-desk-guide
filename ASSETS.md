# Photos and videos: what to get, and how to check you're allowed to use them

## What each guide needs

| Guide | Photos or video you need |
|---|---|
| Record crate | One photo of a crate or box of records, taken straight on; photos of record sleeves (or use blank sleeves with handwriting) |
| Hover-to-spin object | A short video (2 to 6 seconds, ideally looping) of one object on a plain white background |
| Magazine rack | One straight-on photo of a shelf or ledge with a visible front lip. Covers come from your own Substack |
| Arrange mode | Any cut-out photos you want on the desk |

Never ask an AI to generate "photos" of the objects. Fake photos look fake next to real ones. If you don't have a photo yet, leave a plain "Photo needed" box and keep going.

## Cut-outs

An object that sits on your page needs a transparent background. The simple way:

1. Photograph or find the object on a plain, light background.
2. Remove the background so the PNG or WebP is transparent (your assistant can do this by filling in from the corners, or use an image tool). Check the edges at 200% zoom: off-white objects (paper, white sleeves) can get eaten.
3. Save as `.webp` at about 2x the size it will appear.

## Licences, plainly

- **Your own photos:** fine.
- **Stock photos:** check the licence says you can use it on a website and edit it (cutting it out is editing).
- **Photos found online:** assume you cannot use them. Ask the owner or find a licensed one.
- **Anyone else's site, including mine:** copy the approach, never the images or text.

Keep a `CREDITS.md`: one line per image with where it came from and its licence. Fill it in as you go, not at the end.

## Before you make your repo public

Do not put photos in a public repo unless you own them or the licence allows redistribution. Anyone who can see the repo can download them.
